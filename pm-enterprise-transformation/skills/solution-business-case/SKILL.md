# Solution Business Case

## Operating principle

Convert an opportunity into an investment decision. The output must answer:

- should we invest?
- why now?
- what problem and buyer are proven?
- what alternatives exist?
- why are we credible to win?
- how much should we commit now?
- what must be true?
- how will we prove or kill it?

A polished narrative cannot compensate for unsupported economics or weak customer proof.

## Non-negotiable evidence rules

1. Never invent market size, revenue, utilization, margin, client interest, delivery savings, conversion uplift, pricing, customer count, or cost.
2. Label every number as MEASURED, SOURCE-BACKED, ESTIMATE, ASSUMPTION, UNKNOWN, or STALE.
3. Use ranges for uncertain values.
4. If a load-bearing input is unknown, show break-even logic instead of fabricating a point estimate.
5. User-provided claims are evidence only when the user provides or identifies the source-of-truth artifact.
6. A solution cannot be called an accelerator, product, or platform unless reuse and commercialization evidence support that maturity level.
7. Always compare BUILD, BUY, PARTNER, DO NOTHING, services-led delivery, and incumbent/open-source options where relevant.
8. If P0 evidence is missing, recommend EXPERIMENT, HOLD, or NOT READY rather than INVEST.

## Decision frame

Start by stating:

- decision requested
- decision owner/audience
- investment type
- capacity/funding/time ask
- target customer or internal user
- geography/segment
- deadline and reason
- irreversible decision being approached
- explicit non-goals

If this is unclear, output `NOT READY: decision frame incomplete`.

## Business case structure

### 1. Decision requested

Capture:

- exact authorization requested
- budget/capacity/time requested
- what approval enables
- what approval does not enable
- review date
- next gate

### 2. Problem and evidence

Show:

- recurring customer or delivery problem
- workflow pain
- frequency and severity
- current cost of status quo
- source-backed evidence
- open unknowns

Do not use general market excitement as problem proof.

### 3. Customer, buyer, and JTBD

Separate:

- user
- buyer
- economic buyer
- operator
- approver
- blocker
- beneficiary

For each, state evidence status. If unverified, label as hypothesis.

### 4. Strategic fit and right-to-win

Assess:

- adjacency to existing capabilities
- account access
- domain expertise
- reusable assets
- implementation credibility
- differentiated data/process knowledge
- sales/distribution advantage
- strategic value

For each right-to-win claim, ask:

- is it true today?
- is it differentiated?
- does the buyer care?
- can competitors copy it?
- is evidence current?

### 5. Alternatives

Compare:

- BUILD
- BUY
- PARTNER
- DO NOTHING
- SERVICES-led delivery
- OPEN SOURCE/internal toolkit where relevant
- INCUMBENT platform extension where relevant

A BUILD case must beat credible alternatives on evidence-backed dimensions, not preference.

### 6. Market and revenue logic

Avoid TAM theatre.

Separate:

- theoretical market
- serviceable market
- reachable account universe
- current pipeline or account adjacency
- beachhead ICP
- pricing hypothesis
- willingness-to-pay evidence
- sales motion
- sales-cycle implications
- implementation capacity

If reachable market is unknown, calculate break-even account count or usage volume.

### 7. Economics

Model conservative/base/upside:

- build cost
- data/research cost
- implementation cost
- presales cost
- support and maintenance cost
- infrastructure and tooling cost
- human review or operations cost
- expected revenue, gross margin, or savings
- payback
- break-even
- sensitivity to top 3 uncertain drivers

All estimates require formula, units, inputs, and source/assumption labels.

### 8. Execution plan

Use staged commitment:

1. Discovery validation
2. 0-to-1 pilot
3. reusable proof
4. productization or accelerator packaging
5. GTM readiness
6. scale conditions

Do not request platform investment when a narrower proof will answer the next decision.

### 9. Pilot contract

Define:

- design partner/customer criteria
- sample/data requirements
- baseline
- success metric
- guardrail metrics
- false-positive/false-negative risks where relevant
- timeline
- owner
- decision threshold
- kill criteria
- what the pilot cannot prove

A demo is not a pilot. A pilot without a production path is weak proof.

### 10. Risks and failure modes

For each risk:

| Risk | Evidence | Probability/confidence | Impact | Trigger | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not list generic risks. Tie risks to the specific case.

## Red-team gate

Before recommending, answer:

- strongest reason not to invest
- assumption doing the most work
- cheapest alternative
- what happens if demand is half the base case
- what happens if implementation cost is 2x
- what happens if an incumbent bundles the capability
- what evidence would reverse the recommendation
- what should make us stop

## Required output

### Executive decision memo

- Decision: INVEST / PILOT / HOLD / KILL / BUY / PARTNER / NOT READY
- Capacity or funding requested
- Expected value range
- Downside exposure
- Top 3 load-bearing assumptions
- Blocking unknowns
- Cheapest next proof
- Revisit trigger
- Confidence level and reason

### Evidence-backed business case

1. Decision frame
2. Problem and evidence
3. Customer, buyer, and JTBD
4. Why now
5. Alternatives
6. Right-to-win
7. Market/revenue logic
8. Economics and scenarios
9. Execution plan
10. Pilot contract
11. Risks and kill criteria
12. Red-team rejection case
13. Recommendation and next gate

## Hard stop conditions

Return `NOT READY`, `HOLD`, `PILOT`, or `KILL` when:

- problem evidence is weak
- buyer is unknown
- market sizing is unsupported
- right-to-win is aspirational
- alternatives are not compared
- ROI cannot be reconstructed
- pilot cannot fail
- reuse/platform potential is asserted from one project
- sales demand is assumed from internal enthusiasm
- implementation cost or operating burden is unknown and material

## Final self-check

Before delivery, verify:

- I did not invent numbers.
- Every number has a state.
- Alternatives are compared.
- Right-to-win is evidenced.
- Pilot has kill criteria.
- Economics are reconstructable.
- The strongest rejection case is visible.
- Final decision is staged where uncertainty remains.
