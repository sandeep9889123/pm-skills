# AI interaction and trust research

State the user job, participant selection, consent/data restrictions and what the session can establish. Use realistic tasks, including ordinary completion, missing/contradictory evidence, a wrong answer, correction, abstention and human handoff. Do not tell participants the AI is reliable or infer trust from satisfaction alone.

Observe completion, error detection, verification effort, correction/recovery, abandonment, perceived versus actual capability and reviewer burden. Record observed behavior separately from participant statements and researcher interpretation. Keep model assistance, mock responses and live integrations labeled. A prototype preference is not willingness to pay or sustained adoption.

Probe whether users can identify evidence limits, discover controls, correct the system and recover from mistakes. Give a comparable search/rules/manual task when it answers the research question. Avoid leading prompts. Preserve negative cases and sampling limits; do not manufacture a fixed persona count or universal adoption percentage.

Deliver task scripts, observation/evidence locators, trace/variant linkage, success definitions, friction and trust findings, unresolved hypotheses and next validation questions. Combine qualitative findings with permitted cohort/usage evidence without claiming causation from correlation.

Sources: [Microsoft Research human-AI interaction guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/overview/) and [Full Stack LLM Bootcamp language UX material](https://fullstackdeeplearning.com/llm-bootcamp/). The 2023 course is conceptual context, not a source of current API guarantees.
