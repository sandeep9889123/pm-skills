# Prospect Research

Build only the context necessary to improve discovery quality.

## Research dimensions

- company and business model
- geography and regulated context
- customer segments
- likely user/operator/buyer roles
- public strategic priorities
- public products/services and operating model
- technology or integration signals that are actually visible
- industry workflow norms relevant to the proposed problem
- credible alternatives, incumbents, or substitutes
- public evidence of pain, transformation, hiring, investment, or change where available

## Evidence discipline

For every material statement classify:

`FACT | INFERENCE | ASSUMPTION | UNKNOWN | STALE`

Do not infer internal systems merely from industry norms.

Do not say "they use X" because a job posting, vendor case study, or third-party directory weakly suggests it. State the source and confidence.

User-supplied claims are useful leads. They do not become verified facts automatically.

Tool/search failure means `coverage incomplete / UNKNOWN`.

## Research stopping rule

Stop when additional research is unlikely to change:

- problem hypothesis
- use-case ranking
- questions to ask
- expected stakeholders
- system/data dependencies
- proposal-readiness criteria

Do not turn pre-RFP discovery into a full market-research project unless the decision requires it.

## Output

1. account snapshot
2. verified evidence
3. weak signals / inferences
4. unknowns that must be asked live
5. implications for discovery
6. contradictions
7. source gaps
